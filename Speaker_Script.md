# Speaker Script: 7 minutes (SDA-DSC-112 Capstone)

**Big Idea:** Adoption is 66% nationally, but eight regions are below 65%. Four of them hold 87% of the shortfall, and a tiered SAR 40M investment closes the gap.

Rules from the brief: the Ask lands in the first 30 seconds and again at the close. Everyone speaks. The live dashboard is for Q&A only.

Speaker split follows the brief's table (Student 1: slides 1-2, Student 2: slides 3-4, Student 3: slides 5-7). If presenting solo, deliver all slides.

## Slide 1: Ask (0:00-0:30)
"Today we're asking you to approve SAR 40 million for digital adoption. We recommend a tiered allocation: SAR 32 million, 80 percent, to the four regions with the widest gaps, Najran, Northern Borders, Al-Baha and Jazan, and SAR 8 million to close the gap in four regions that are nearly there. Here is why."

## Slide 2: Situation (0:30-1:15)
"Start with the good news. Nationally, Tayseer adoption is 66.2 percent across 13 regions, 1.2 points above our 65 percent target. If we stopped at this number, we'd conclude nothing needs doing. One caveat: our dashboard shows adoption today, not a trend, so 'on track' means 'at target today'."

## Slide 3: Complication (1:15-2:00)
"But the national average hides the problem. Green bars are at or above target, red bars are below. Eight of our 13 regions are red. Together they are 12.2 percentage points short, and Najran is the furthest behind, 4.1 points."

## Slide 4: Evidence (2:00-4:00)
"Look at the gap to target for the eight red regions. Najran 4.1, Northern Borders 2.3, Al-Baha 2.2, Jazan 2.0: together 10.6 of the 12.2 points, that is 87 percent of the shortfall. The other four, Al-Jouf, Hail, Tabuk and Asir, are each within 0.6 points. So these are two different problems: four regions need real investment, four need a small push."

## Slide 5: Options (4:00-5:15)
"We compared three ways to spend the SAR 40 million. Option A, spread evenly, SAR 5 million each: fair, but Al-Jouf, 0.2 points short, gets as much as Najran, 4.1 points short, about 20 times more money per point of gap. Option B, SAR 10 million each to the widest four: it leaves four regions below target. Option C, tiered: SAR 32 million weighted to the widest four and SAR 2 million to each of the rest. These are illustrative allocations built from the dashboard gaps only."

## Slide 6: Recommendation (5:15-6:15)
"We recommend Option C. SAR 12.5 million to Najran, 7 to Northern Borders, 6.5 to Al-Baha, 6 to Jazan, and 2 million each to the other four. That is about SAR 3 million per point of gap in the widest four. It closes the biggest gaps first and leaves no lagging region behind."

## Slide 7: Ask + next step (6:15-7:00)
"So the decision we need today: approve the SAR 40 million tiered allocation. Within 30 days, we validate cost per point of adoption with each region's delivery team. At six months, we re-check the dashboard: are all eight regions at or above 65 percent? Thank you, we're happy to take questions."

## Q&A preparation
Open the live Tableau dashboard if a question needs it. Acknowledge uncertainty, then return to the Big Idea.

- **Why 80/20?** The widest four hold 87% of the shortfall; 80% is close to that share, with a small buffer so near-miss regions are not ignored. It is a judgment call, not a model output.
- **Is SAR 3M per point real?** It is derived from our illustrative split, not measured cost. That is why validation is step 2.
- **Why not just Option B?** It leaves Al-Jouf, Hail, Tabuk and Asir below 65% for a small amount of money.
- **National is already above 65%. Why spend?** The target is regional equity; eight regions are still below it, and the average masks that.
- **What about the trend?** Not in the dashboard. A time series would show whether lagging regions are improving on their own, and is a recommended follow-up.
- **How did you calculate the gap?** 65 minus current regional adoption, in percentage points.
